Retaining Walls Sunshine Coast Questions to Ask Explained

Approval questions come first because they change the whole job. The Sunshine Coast Council requires a building permit before construction, modification or repair of most retaining walls, and council approval as a concurrence agency applies for walls over 2 metres. Before a contractor treats the work as a simple landscaping item, ask:

  • Does this wall need a building permit, and who lodges it?
  • Does the height or the load above the wall trigger engineering?
  • Who provides Form 15 design certification and Form 16 inspection certification?
  • Is council concurrence required, and is that cost included?

Walls over 1 metre, or any wall carrying a surcharge such as a driveway or building, move into an engineered pathway under AS 4678 with certification by a Registered Professional Engineer of Queensland. That engineering sits outside a basic material price, which is why two quotes for the same wall face can look very different.

Questions About Ground, Height and Load

The same wall face behaves differently on a Blackall Range slope near Maleny, a Buderim volcanic soil site, a Landsborough sandstone block or canal fill at Maroochydore. Ask the contractor how they assessed the ground before pricing, and what they expect the soil to do behind the wall. Useful questions include:

  • Have you allowed for the soil type and water movement on this site?
  • Is this a new wall, a replacement, a repair or a modification?
  • Is this low garden retaining or a structural retaining job?
  • How does access to the site affect the cost?

Measure the wall face yourself before requesting quotes. Face area is height multiplied by length, so a 1.2 metre wall that is 10 metres long is 12 square metres. Describing the job in square metres of face area is more useful than asking for a price per linear metre.

Questions About Materials and Fit

Common wall types include concrete sleeper, timber sleeper, besser block, rock and boulder, gabion, plus drainage systems, engineered walls and repairs. The better question is not which material sounds strongest but which suits the height, ground, finish and space behind the wall:

  • Concrete sleepers between galvanised posts suit cut-and-fill blocks and replacement work.
  • Timber sleeper terraces suit low garden falls.
  • Core-filled besser block suits taller engineered jobs and rendered finishes.
  • Rock walls need room to batter back into the slope.
  • Gabions suit scour and surface runoff on erodible ground.

Ask each contractor why their nominated material fits this particular site, not just the wall in general. Questions About Drainage and Failure Points

Drainage is part of the structure, not a finishing detail. Poor drainage is the leading cause of wall failure, so a quote that omits drainage detail cannot be compared fairly with one that names the backfill, drain line, geofabric and discharge approach. Ask:

  • Is ag drain, aggregate and geofabric included behind the wall?
  • Where does collected water go once it reaches the drain?
  • Is drainage included behind every wall type, not just engineered walls?
  • For repairs, will you assess the existing drainage failure point?

This applies equally on wet hinterland slopes and coastal dune sand. Water behind the wall must be managed as part of the wall design, and the discharge question matters because a drain that exits onto a neighbour's boundary or back toward the footing creates a new problem.

Questions About the Quote Itself

Finally, separate inclusions from exclusions in writing. Indicative face-area costs run from around $150 to $300 per square metre for rock and $300 to $450 for timber sleepers, but those rates rarely capture the whole job. Ask each contractor:

  • What sits outside the wall-face rate, such as engineering, approvals, access and removal of the old wall?
  • How are variations priced if the ground differs from what was assumed?
  • What certification and inspection documents will be handed over at completion?

Who These Questions Apply To

These questions suit owners planning a new wall, replacing a failing wall, modifying an existing wall, or working out whether a low garden fall has become a structural retaining job. They are most relevant where height, slope, load above the wall or council approval could change the scope, and they give quote comparisons a common basis so price differences reflect real differences in work.

  1. Measure the wall face. Record the height and length of the wall, then multiply them to describe the job in square metres of face area.
  2. Describe the load. Note any driveway, building, slope, fence or other surcharge above the wall before requesting a quote.
  3. Check approval status. Ask whether the job needs a building permit, council concurrence or engineered certification under AS 4678.
  4. Compare drainage scopes. Look for ag drain, aggregate, geofabric and a discharge approach in every quote.
  5. Separate inclusions from exclusions. Ask which costs sit outside the wall-face rate, including engineering, approvals, access and removal.

Common questions

Do most retaining walls on the Sunshine Coast need a building permit? Yes. A building permit is required before construction, modification or repair of most retaining walls, and Sunshine Coast Council approval as a concurrence agency applies for walls over 2 metres.

When does a retaining wall need engineering certification? Walls over 1 metre high, or walls carrying a surcharge such as a driveway or building, follow an engineered pathway using AS 4678 with Form 15 design certification and Form 16 inspection certification by a Registered Professional Engineer of Queensland.

What is the biggest cause of retaining wall failure? Poor drainage. Subsoil drainage with ag drain, aggregate and geofabric is part of the wall structure, so any quote should state the backfill, drain line and where collected water discharges.
